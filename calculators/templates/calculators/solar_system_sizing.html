BEFORE YOU BEGIN

A few helpful tips

  1. Know your daily demand. Use watt-hours (Wh), not watts (W). Build a load worksheet.
  2. Choose realistic sunlight. Peak sun hours are equivalent full-sun hours, not daylight duration.
  3. Plan for cloudy days. Battery autonomy is the number of days you want stored energy to cover.

Calculation Assumptions

The solar-array estimate divides daily energy use by peak sun hours and overall system efficiency. Peak sun hours are equivalent full-sun hours, not total daylight hours.

Usable battery energy equals daily energy use multiplied by battery autonomy days. Nominal battery energy divides that requirement by the usable battery capacity percentage; amp-hours divide nominal watt-hours by battery-bank voltage.

The system efficiency input adjusts solar production only. It is not applied a second time to the battery estimate. Enter battery autonomy based on the energy demand your battery must actually supply; account separately for any battery/inverter losses not already reflected in your load estimate.

This is a planning estimate. Actual sizing should account for seasonal solar availability, surge loads, equipment specifications, battery chemistry, temperature, and installation-specific losses.
